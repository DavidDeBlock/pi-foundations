## 1. Money & Margins: From "Time and Materials" to the Menu Card

### The Bottlenecks

- **Hourly Rate Trap:** Working by the hour penalizes you for being fast and skilled (less revenue). In addition, you often don't dare to charge the actual time when a job is difficult due to rust or dirt.
    
- **The Wreck Dilemma:** Bikes with severe lack of maintenance (everything worn out: chain, sprockets, tires, cables) suddenly drive the invoice up to €300 to €400. Out of guilt, you give away hours for free ("fake busyness").
    
- **Margin-eating Transactions:** Cash is disappearing, and debit card (Bancontact) transaction fees eat into your margin on small repairs.
    

### The System Solutions

- **Flat-Rate Pricing (Garage System):** The database works with fixed prices per task (average wrenching time + difficulty) instead of counting hours. Speed is finally rewarded.
    
- **The Phasing Button:** You split up big jobs in the software. Today you only do **Phase 1 (Safety)** such as brakes and tires (€180). **Phase 2 (Comfort)** such as the chain or a broken saddle (€220) is parked in the system for 3 months from now. This gives you constant work security and the customer a manageable invoice.
    
- **Neglected Maintenance Surcharge & Warranty Exclusion:** A checkmark for "Neglected" directly adds a fixed cleaning/unblocking surcharge (e.g., €25). Do you perform a repair only partially (Phase 1 only)? Then the system automatically attaches a strict **warranty exclusion clause** to the invoice for the unreplaced parts. No arguments afterwards.
    
- **The "Total Loss" Notification:** Does the amount exceed a pre-set limit (e.g., €150)? Then the work order blocks, and the system automatically sends an approval SMS to the customer. Work can only resume after their digital click.
    
- **Invisible Transaction Indexing:** Electronic payment transaction fees are standardly factored into the fixed task prices as a percentage.
    

## 2. Time & Focus: Peace in the Workshop

### The Bottlenecks

- **The Clutter Annoyance:** You are constantly pulled away from complex diagnostics by walk-ins asking for a loose screw, a new saddle, or a tire that needs pumping.
    
- **Review Hostage:** The fear of a bad 1-star review makes you help these walk-ins quickly and for free, at the expense of your own energy and revenue.
    

### The System Solutions

- **Split Opening Hours (The Focus Day):** You set your opening hours strictly and link the online module to them:
    
    - **08:30 - 10:00 | Open for Walk-ins & Quick-Fix:** Customers bring in their 'Repair Day' bikes, and walk-ins are welcome for minor interventions.
        
    - **10:00 - 14:00 | Shop CLOSED (Focus Block):** The doorbell and phone are turned off. You and your employee wrench non-stop and without interruption.
        
    - **14:00 - 16:30 | New Bikes by Appointment:** Customers book a 45-minute block online for buying advice. No walk-ins, 100% focus on sales.
        
    - **16:30 - 18:30 | Open for Pickup & Quick-Fix:** Bikes are picked up, and the system automatically sends payment requests.
        
- **The Quick-Fix Filter:** Small tasks cannot be booked online. If someone still tries to squeeze in outside of walk-in hours? Then your register relentlessly charges a **minimum rate of €15 for 10 minutes of work**. Freeloaders filter themselves out immediately.
    
- **Self-Service Tire Pump:** Hang an automatic compressor outside or near the door. Regular customers (who bought their bike from you) get a free token; walk-ins pay via a donation jar.
    

## 3. Space: Master of the Square Meters

### The Bottlenecks

- **Physical Bottleneck:** You can store a maximum of 40 to 50 bikes (including large cargo bikes that take up the space of 3 standard bikes).
    
- **Warehouse Congestion:** If customers "forget" their finished bikes (holiday, illness) or if you have to wait for parts, your entire shop physically clogs up, and the schedule stalls.
    

### The System Solutions

- **Live Capacity Widget:** You build an indicator on your dashboard. Every bike with the status "In", "In Repair", "Waiting for Parts", or "Ready" counts. If the counter goes above 40 bikes (Red), the online module automatically scales back bookings for the coming days immediately.
    
- **The "Drop-off Day" Logic:** Customers book a _repair day_, not a specific hour. The instruction is clear: "Drop off the bike before 09:00 AM." Your dashboard only physically adds +1 once you actually load and scan the bike.
    
- **Automated Pickup Reminders:** The system sends fully automated messages: **Day 1** (Bike is ready!), **Day 3** (Friendly reminder), and **Day 7** (Warning that storage fees may be charged in accordance with the general terms and conditions).
    
- **Minimum Work Buffer:** The system monitors a minimum threshold of work hours for your employee. If there is a risk of downtime, the dashboard automatically pulls forward "flexible tasks" (bikes that only need to be ready by the end of the week).
    

## 4. Local Online Counter: Click & Collect for Parts

### The Bottlenecks

- **Time-wasters at the counter:** DIYers who stop by and demand that you search through complex catalogs and systems for a single specific bolt or brake pad.
    
- **Price Hunters:** You don't want a public webshop, because competitors and walk-ins can then too easily scan and compare your prices online.
    

### The System Solutions

- **Closed Catalog:** You build the parts module behind a mandatory customer login. Only real, local customers get access to the digital menu card with market-conforming prices.
    
- **The Doubt Button (Photo Upload):** If a DIYer is not sure which part they need, instead of discussing it at the counter, they upload a photo of their current part via the module. You look at these during a quiet moment in your focus block, select the correct part, and the system pushes the payment link.
    
- **Pickup Logic with Upsell:** Customers pay online, but only receive an SMS that they can pick up their order once you actually pull the part from the shelf and scan it. Naturally, this only happens during walk-in hours. Upon pickup, the system prompts a smart pop-up for your employee: _"Does the customer also need chain lube or the correct cassette tool?"_
